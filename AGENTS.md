## Scope and evidence

Documentation/archive acceptance concerns bytes, source accuracy, local links,
provenance and redistribution boundaries. It does not require Android execution.
Offline movie acceptance concerns the real mathematical state, frames, replay,
encoder output and independent verification. It does not establish device behavior.

For application and device claims, require independent exact-head mathematical,
renderer, Android and delivery receipts applicable to the claimed result.
Physical A1/C67 behavior and replacement updates remain separate from host/emulator
evidence. Never claim completion of pending Griess book operations from generic
signed-algebra tests. Do not guess illegible arrows, coordinate labels or signs.

## Movie ownership

Mathieu owns mathematical actions, interaction/session history, replay clocks,
labels/shapes/colors/layouts and source frames. Use the existing native engine,
session and renderer; never replace group actions with numerical root animations.

Kitchen alone owns movie encoding. Do not implement/copy a private FFmpeg encoder,
raw-RGB video wrapper or MP4 packager here. The capture entrypoint delegates real
planned movies to the existing FP WIGGLE operation and independent AI-CI verifier.
Its compatibility validator may check its own inputs and invoke the unchanged
pinned Kitchen builder. Keep codec/muxing changes and their tests in Kitchen.

Produce contiguous numbered source frames beginning at zero, with their hashes,
authored motion plan, exact action/session state and source identities retained.
Controls must be independently suppressible in the application renderer.
An offline film never proves that Android displayed those frames.

Existing source/runtime boundaries are explicit: the Ithon host substrate and
FP legacy controller are inherited upstream debt, not permission to generate
new consumer Python. Do not substitute another runtime to bypass Ithon checking.
